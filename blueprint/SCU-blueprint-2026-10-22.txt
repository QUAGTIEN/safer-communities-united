PART I — WEBSITE FOUNDATION
1. WEBSITE GOALS
The Safer Communities United website aims to:
* Show residents that the current Community Plan is causing problems and consequences for the health, safety, environment, and welfare of Keystone–Odessa.
* Clearly present the issues related to roads, water and septic, infrastructure, growth, housing, and access to essential services.
* Demonstrate these issues through data, maps, public records, official documents, and the actual conditions of the community.
* Show the connection between Community Plan rules → actual conditions → consequences for people and the environment.
* Compare the Keystone–Odessa Community Plan with the Hillsborough County Comprehensive Plan to clearly show the differences in policies, planning tools, and the ability to address current issues.
* Present the proposal to remove the current Community Plan and move to an appropriate countywide planning framework.
* Gather community support after residents understand the issues, the evidence, the differences between the two planning frameworks, and the direction of the proposed change.
Core Flow:
Problem → Consequences → Evidence → Comparison → Solution → Action

The website should have a local-community style that is serious, modern, and evidence-based.
The design should be clean, spacious, easy to read, and create a sense of trust.
Prioritize large amounts of white space, limited text on each screen, real images, maps, data, and official records. Each section should focus on only one main message.

* Card Heading: the name of an issue or piece of evidence.
* Small Label: used for groups such as ISSUES · DATA · PUBLIC RECORDS · COMPARISON.
* Body Content: short, preferably 1–3 sentences for each block.
Long paragraphs are reserved for detailed pages.

Maps: include a title, clear legend, and source; highlight only the data layers needed for the message being presented.
Public Records: display an image or visual excerpt of the document, together with the date, agency, key content, and a link to the original document.
Sources: place each source close to the fact it supports, but keep the presentation compact so it does not make the interface look cluttered.
White Space and Layout
Pages must be clean, spacious, and easy to scan.
* One section focuses on one message.
* Do not place too many cards on the same screen.
* Do not use long paragraphs on overview pages.

* Keep the main content within a comfortable reading width. Avoid spreading text across the full width of the screen.

A longer drive for groceries may be a choice you’re happy to make. But protecting rural character should never be an excuse to dismiss safe roads, essential services or responsible infrastructure. Loving Keystone–Odessa does not mean treating its Community Plan as untouchable.

ISSUES › ROADS & SAFETY
01 — STREET LIGHTING
Street lighting should ↵ respond to public ↵ safety needs.
Does someone have to get hurt first?
The Community Plan takes a restrictive approach to street lighting and allows lighting when public safety is at risk.
CURRENT COMMUNITY PLAN RULE
The Community Plan takes a restrictive approach to street lighting and allows lighting when public safety is at risk.
“Street lighting should not be
required nor permitted unless
public safety is at risk.”
— Keystone–Odessa Community Plan
View the Full Policy in the Community Plan →
ACTUAL SAFETY NEEDS
Adequate street lighting is important for:
Visibility for drivers
Intersections and high-risk areas
Pedestrians and cyclists
Night travel and daily commutes
WHY IT MATTERS
Limited street lighting can make it more difficult and dangerous to travel at night, increasing the risk of crashes and affecting the safety of residents, students, commuters and emergency responders.
It directly impacts the health, safety and welfare of our community.
FHWA explains that modern lighting can precisely target where light is needed while minimizing spill into the night sky and neighboring properties. Rural character should drive better design, not end the safety discussion.
Source: Federal Highway Administration Lighting safety countermeasure. FHWA-SA-21-050, Applications and Considerations. Modern lighting control and reduced spill; general engineering guidance.
Read FHWA guidance on lighting

Expansion of Gunn Highway ↵ is restricted.

WHY IT MATTERS
Limits on expanding Gunn Highway affect traffic, safety and access for residents, commuters, students and emergency services.
19,912 DAILY VEHICLE MOVEMENTS. TWO LANES.
The County’s 2024 data put Gunn Highway from the Pasco County line to Van Dyke Road at 19,912 annual-average daily vehicle movements. Both daily and peak-direction service are rated E against a D standard. These figures demand a serious response.

03 — WALKING, BIKING & CROSSINGS
Pedestrians and cyclists ↵ face safety risks.
The safety of pedestrians, cyclists, and people crossing the road needs to be considered together with traffic, speed, crossings and the actual conditions on each corridor.
COMMUNITY PLAN RULE
Is “when possible” enough for safe, connected routes?
The Plan directs the County to encourage pedestrian and equestrian trails along designated scenic corridors. Off-road trails and bikeways in preserved greenways are to be developed when possible, without a delivery timetable in these provisions.
Policy summary · Livable Communities Element, p. 25
View the Relevant Policy in the Community Plan →
ACTUAL CONDITIONS
Traffic and higher speeds
Limited pedestrian and bicycle facilities
Safety at intersections and crossings
Continuous routes are limited
WHY IT MATTERS
Limited and unsafe conditions for pedestrians and cyclists increase the risk of crashes and affect the safety of residents, students, commuters and everyone who uses these roads.
Safe walking and biking routes are essential for a safer, healthier and more connected community.
SAFE WALKING SHOULD NOT BE AN EXCEPTION.
County Mobility Objective 2.2 calls for protecting pedestrians, children, seniors and people with disabilities. FHWA recommends connected walking routes without gaps. A safe route matters whether you are going to school, visiting a neighbor or using a wheelchair.
Sources: County Comprehensive Plan, Mobility Objective 2.2 and Policies 2.2.1–2.2.7, p. 127; FHWA Walkways, FHWA-SA-21-047, connected routes and accessibility.

ISSUES › ROADS & SAFETY › EMERGENCY ACCESS
04 — EMERGENCY ACCESS
Emergency access ↵ must be reliable.
Emergency access should be judged by evidence, not by a fixed preference for road size. The Community Plan accepts that safety may require change, yet narrows the options for making it.
WHAT THE PLAN SAYS
Safety changes are allowed. The road options are constrained.
The plan favors turn lanes, crossings and traffic controls instead of widening, and says Gunn Highway cannot be widened further.
Summary: Community Plan, p. 24
It also allows lighting when public safety is at risk and supports widening the Suncoast Parkway.
View the Relevant Policy in the Community Plan →
KEY FACTORS
Emergency response time
Intersections and road design
Congestion and delays
Road conditions
Access during severe weather
WHY IT MATTERS
Reliable emergency access is essential for residents, students, commuters and the entire community. Delays can affect emergency response times and public safety.
Road decisions should support faster, safer emergency access for the community.

07 — HOW DO THE COMMUNITY PLAN AND COMPREHENSIVE PLAN APPROACH THE ISSUE?
Two planning frameworks. Two approaches to roads and safety.
KEYSTONE–ODESSA COMMUNITY PLAN
Rural road characteristics
Should a rural appearance come before people’s daily needs? The plan prioritizes two-lane rural roads and limits changes that increase capacity or urbanize the area. (Community Plan, Transportation)
Road configuration
Why limit the options before assessing what people need? The plan identifies preferred road configurations, including two-lane rural sections, and the types of improvements considered appropriate for the area.
Gunn Highway
How much traffic is enough to reconsider the restrictions? Gunn Highway is identified as a road with restrictions on further expansion and included in the constrained-road tables. (Community Plan, related policies)
Lighting
Does someone have to get hurt first?
The plan allows lighting when public safety is at risk.
Identify the danger. Act before someone is injured.
Transportation improvements
Should a safety improvement have to pass a rural-character test? The plan allows intersection, cycling, walking and traffic-control improvements while requiring rural character to be maintained.
HILLSBOROUGH COUNTY COMPREHENSIVE PLAN
Transportation safety
Includes countywide policies to improve transportation safety, reduce crashes, and address safety for all users, including drivers, pedestrians and cyclists. (Mobility Section)
Mobility
Supports a connected and functional multimodal network, including roadways, transit, bicycle and pedestrian facilities. (Mobility Section)
Infrastructure needs
Addresses transportation infrastructure needs based on current and future conditions, including roadway capacity, intersection improvements and system performance. (Mobility Section)
Evaluation of conditions
Uses data, level-of-service standards and network evaluation to identify needed improvements across the county. (Mobility Section)
Planning tools
Provides broader transportation planning tools, such as the Cost Feasible Plan, the Transportation Improvement Program (TIP) and coordination with the Long Range Transportation Plan (LRTP). (Mobility Section)
This is a summary comparison. The detailed analysis of each policy, with side-by-side excerpts from both plans, is on the Comparison Page.
